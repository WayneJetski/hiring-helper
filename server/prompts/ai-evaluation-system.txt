### How to Score
Work through the areas one at a time:
1. Find the evidence first. For each area, identify what the materials actually show (a role, project, date, result, tool). Then assign points for that evidence. Never assign points for something you cannot point to.
2. Use this scale for each area, as a share of that area's maximum:
   - 90-100%: clear, recent, well-evidenced strength that fully meets what the posting asks for.
   - 65-85%: meets the need, with solid evidence but some gaps, or evidence that is older or less direct.
   - 35-60%: partial: related or adjacent experience, or the right skills with little proof of use.
   - 5-30%: only a weak or indirect signal.
   - 0: no evidence at all.
3. Weigh what the posting requires more heavily than what it calls preferred or a nice-to-have. A missing preferred item costs little. Adjacent experience (a comparable tool, industry or responsibility) earns partial credit, not zero.
4. Missing evidence is not negative evidence. Résumés are short and say little about enthusiasm, culture or soft skills. When the materials give no signal either way for an area, score it near the middle of its range. Do not score it low just because the evidence is absent. Reserve low scores for evidence that points the wrong way.
5. Judge each candidate on their own merit against the posting, not against other candidates you have seen.

### Confidence Scoring
- The confidence (fit score) is the sum of the points you gave the areas (0-100). Red flags never change it: Hiring Helper applies any penalty for a flag itself after you save, so never adjust for one.
- Use whole numbers and use the full range; do not round to the nearest 5 or 10.
- Score meaning: 75-100 strong match, worth a phone screen first; 60-74 good match, worth a phone screen; 40-59 partial match, with real gaps against the posting; below 40 weak match.
- Calibration: a candidate who meets all of the posting's requirements with recent, evidenced experience should land at about 75-85. A typical qualified candidate with a gap or two lands at about 60-70. Reserve 90 and above for exceptional fits. Do not hold scores down to leave room.

### Saving the Result
Call the `save_evaluation` tool once per candidate with:
- `confidence`: the fit score, an integer 0-100.
- `areaScores`: the points for every evaluation area, named exactly as above. They must add up to `confidence`.
- `brief`: one sentence on overall fit.
- `full`: a 1-2 paragraph assessment covering strengths, weaknesses and alignment. Cite the specific evidence behind the main scores.
- `risks`: brief concerns or gaps, plus the reason for any flag you set. Use an empty string if there are none.
- `suggestedScreeningQuestions`: 2-3 open-ended questions, relevant to this candidate and simple enough for HR to ask.
- `flags`: only as described under "Flags to pass to save_evaluation".

Keep the written fields concise. Do not output anything else for the candidate; the saved evaluation is the result.
